01

What customers actually call the problem

The single most useful output of this research is the vocabulary map, because your working name for the product — "3D packaging visualization" — is a phrase almost nobody types. packaging visualization returns 10/mo in the US and 0/mo in India. Observed

Instead, four distinct vocabularies exist, and each belongs to a different person:

1 · The designer says "mockup"

packaging mockup, pouch mockup, box mockup, mockup psd, free mockup. Wants a free PSD file. Volume: enormous. Money: near zero.

2 · The structural designer says "dieline"

dieline (US 3,600 · IN 1,900), box template, packaging dieline, tuck box. Wants correct geometry, not a render. This is Pacdora's actual core.

3 · The software buyer says "configurator"

3d product configurator, 3d configurator software, product customizer, web to print. Small volume, huge CPC, unambiguous purchase intent.

4 · The brand ops buyer says "artwork management"

artwork management software, online proofing software, packaging artwork management, prepress software. Compliance language, not visual language.

Key vocabulary finding

Nobody searches the problem. I ran phrase_questions on "packaging mockup": the entire question set is 20/mo or less and every single item is a Photoshop or Illustrator how-to — "how to make packaging mockup in photoshop", "how to model a pouch package mockup in blender". There is no "see my packaging before printing" search behaviour to capture. Observed Negative

The strategic consequence is large: you cannot win this market with problem-aware SEO, because the problem is not searched. Inference Acquisition has to come from category terms, competitor terms, or outbound.

Market gaps — the wedge candidates

  1. No credible flexible-packaging 3D configurator exists. Every configurator vendor found (Threekit, Zakeke, Expivi, Cylindo, VividWorks, Simplio3D, Dopple) is furniture, apparel, or general ecommerce. Pacdora's US top pages contain zero pouch/flexible content. Flexible packaging is soft-body geometry — genuinely harder than a box — which is both the gap and the moat. Observed
  2. Enterprise tools are unreachable and unusable. Esko owns the category and gets 6,917 US organic visits/month. ArtiosCAD requires training. There is no self-serve tier between "free PSD" and "call our sales team."
  3. Free tools stop at the render. Pacdora, Placeit, Canva output an image. None produce a quote, a purchase order, or a print-ready file. The commercial handoff is the gap.
  4. Dieline → 3D is manual everywhere. dieline 3,600/mo US and convert dieline to 3d has no measurable volume — meaning the conversion is happening but nobody has named it as a product.
  5. Nobody has built the configurator for the packaging vertical specifically. Generalists win on breadth; you can win on format library depth (pouch gussets, spouts, zippers, gravure vs digital substrate finishes) that a generalist will never build.

Features required to sell — and what can wait

Must exist before you can sell

  • Embeddable widget — a script tag or iframe on the converter's own quote page. Without this there is no product for ICP 1.
  • Artwork upload that survives real files — PDF, AI, and EPS with spot colours and bleed, not just PNG.
  • Correct dieline-to-3D mapping for flexible formats — stand-up pouch with gusset, flat pouch, sachet, quad-seal, coffee bag with valve. If the wrap is visibly wrong, a packaging professional dismisses it in three seconds.
  • Believable film finishes — matte, gloss, metallized, kraft, spot UV, clear window. This is the credibility test for this industry.
  • Quote handoff — the configured spec plus the artwork arrives in the converter's inbox or CRM. This is the feature that makes it a revenue tool rather than a toy.
  • Shareable link — the buyer's internal stakeholders must be able to view it without an account.

Can wait

  • Photorealistic ray-traced rendering — "clearly correct" beats "beautiful" for a quote-page decision
  • AR / mobile viewer
  • Rigid formats (boxes, cartons, bottles, jars, tubes) — Pacdora already owns box templates; do not open that front early
  • Animation and turntable video export
  • Approval workflows, versioning, comment threads — that is the enterprise artwork-management product, a different company
  • AI design generation
  • A full free public mockup gallery — worth building as an SEO asset in month 6+, not before revenue
  • Anything India-specific

Negative findings — what not to pursue

  1. Do not build for the free-mockup audience as a revenue segment. The pouch mockup SERP contains zero SaaS products — it is free-PSD sites, Envato, Pinterest, and Canva. That audience has revealed its price. Negative
  2. Do not launch in India. 10–20× lower CPC, near-zero B2B software search, and a mockup audience composed of local-print freelancers searching for visiting-card and standee templates.
  3. Do not target students or tutorial audiences. vidIQ shows the YouTube audience for this topic is BD 31% / IN 29% / PK 11% on packaging design — a learner population, not a buyer population.
  4. Do not chase the "packaging visualization software" category. It does not exist in search. 10/mo US, 0/mo India. There is no category to own because no one is asking for it.
  5. Do not compete with Pacdora on dielines or box templates. It holds position 1 on box template, blind box template, cereal box template, dielines, backed by ~440K monthly visits.
  6. Do not build enterprise artwork approval. Esko, Loftware, GlobalVision, Dalim, TraceOne are entrenched, the buyer wants regulatory proofreading rather than 3D, and the sales cycle is 9–18 months.
  7. Do not build a general-purpose mockup generator. The "mockup" universe is apparel and merch — t-shirt, hoodie, hat, tote. Placeit, Smartmockups and Pacdora already serve it, and Pacdora is actively pivoting into apparel. Nothing about packaging expertise wins there.
  8. Do not assume the physical-sample pain. You listed it; I looked for it. packaging samples 70/mo, packaging prototype 70/mo. If this pain is real it is being handled through supplier relationships and is invisible to search — so validate it in interviews, not in a keyword tool. Negative
24

Unexpected findings

1 · Pacdora is not a packaging company

The category leader's US top pages are a barcode generator, an AI background remover, an envelope template, and apparel mockups. It reached ~440K visits/month by farming free-utility SEO, then pivoted where the mockup money actually is: clothing. If you copy "Pacdora for pouches," you will copy the wrong half of Pacdora.

2 · "3D packaging" is small; "configurator" is where the money is

Exactly the inversion you suspected might exist. 3d packaging mockup: 40/mo @ $2.61. 3d configurator software: 320/mo @ $22.38. Same technology, 8.5× the click value.

3 · Enterprise packaging software has almost no SEO

Esko — the dominant vendor on earth — gets 6,917 US organic visits/month. Threekit 3,237. Zakeke 2,838. This category is not searched into; it is sold into. That kills the SEO-led plan and simultaneously makes outbound far more viable than it looks.

4 · India has 11× the volume and 1/12th the value

pouch mockup: IN 1,600 vs US 140, at $0.22 vs $2.72. The instinct to launch where the searches are would have been exactly backwards.

5 · The word "packaging" is unusable in consumer search

Broad-match "packaging" questions in the US return how to track a package (110,000/mo), where is my package (40,500), nfl ticket packages, optimum internet packages. Any top-of-funnel plan built on the bare word "packaging" is measuring parcel tracking and cable bundles.

6 · Packlane validates the business model outright

2,900/mo brand search at a $12.01 CPC. A company willing to pay $12 for a click on its own name has proven that customised-packaging-with-preview generates real LTV. That is the closest thing to third-party proof of your thesis in this entire dataset — and it comes from boxes, not pouches.
