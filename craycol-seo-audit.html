The two critical findings

Critical Performance Every page view

The homepage downloads 26 MB of video — and mobile downloads both the mobile and the desktop copy

There are two <video> elements in the hero banner. Both are set to autoplay with preload="auto", which forces the browser to pull the entire file before it can settle. On a phone, the desktop video is hidden by CSS — but it is still fully downloaded.

The two files are 13.0 MB (c6c1650d…mp4, desktop) and 12.8 MB (49f2d0ab…mp4, mobile). For context, a landing page should sit under 1 MB total. A customer in Pune on 4G is paying for roughly 26 MB of data before they see a product — and Google measures Core Web Vitals from exactly those real-world sessions.

One piece of good news: layout stability is excellent (CLS 0.001). The problem is purely payload.

Fix Set preload="none" on both videos and add a lightweight poster image so the hero paints instantly. Load only the variant that matches the viewport — swap the src in with JS behind a matchMedia check, rather than rendering both and hiding one. Then re-encode: these should be 1–2 MB each at H.264/WebM, not 13 MB. Expect mobile load to drop from ~16 s to under 3 s.

How you’ll know it worked: mobile page weight under 3 MB in DevTools, and the LCP element becomes the poster image or headline instead of the video.

What this actually risks

Google’s SpamBrain generally ignores links like these rather than penalising the target — most of the time, bought links simply do nothing. But the volume here is unusual relative to the site’s size, the anchor text is a commercial sales pitch rather than anything topical, and gambling and adult domains are in the mix. That combination is what manual actions get issued for.

The honest read: this is more likely wasted money than an active penalty right now. It is still worth cleaning up, because the downside is a manual action that takes months to lift.

Fix First, find out who bought these and stop the campaign — otherwise the disavow file goes stale within weeks. Then export the full referring-domain list from Search Console (Links → Top linking sites), tag every domain matching the patterns above, and submit a domain-level disavow file. Do not attempt outreach removal with link vendors; disavow is the right tool.

How you’ll know it worked: check Search Console → Security & Manual Actions first. If it says “No issues detected,” you are cleaning up preventatively and should expect no ranking change — that is the correct outcome, not a failed fix.

The one gap worth closing

Bing Webmaster Tools is not set up. That matters more than it used to: Microsoft Copilot and a share of ChatGPT’s web results are served from the Bing index. Verification takes about five minutes and you can import the site straight from Search Console. It also unlocks IndexNow, which pushes new products to Bing within minutes instead of waiting for a crawl.

One thing to check inside the tools rather than from outside: whether Google Ads and Meta are running server-side conversion tracking (Conversions API). It isn’t visible from the browser. Without it, iOS conversion data is materially under-reported, which quietly makes your ad ROAS look worse than it is.

High-priority fixes

HighStructured data

Every product page ships two conflicting Product schemas

Product pages contain two separate Product JSON-LD blocks — one from the theme, one from an app — with different offer structures and price formats. There are also two Organization blocks, one of which lists eight empty strings in its sameAs array. Google has to guess which is authoritative, and duplicate entities are a known cause of rich results silently not appearing.

Missing entirely across all 69 pages crawled: BreadcrumbList (0 pages), and ItemList or CollectionPage on all 21 collection pages.

Fix Pick one source of truth — keep the theme’s block, disable the app’s duplicate — and strip the empty sameAs entries. Add BreadcrumbList sitewide and ItemList to collections. Validate in Google’s Rich Results Test before and after.
HighImagesAccessibility

38% of images have no alt attribute at all

Across the pages crawled: 5,407 <img> tags, of which 2,045 carry no alt attribute and a further 933 have an empty alt="". Empty alt is correct for decorative images; a missing attribute is not. For an eyewear brand this is a direct loss — Google Images is a real discovery channel for sunglasses, and alt text is how product photos qualify.

Fix Set alt text at the Shopify media level so it propagates everywhere the image is used. Describe the product concretely — “Craycol Kasa Kai blue reflective polarised sport sunglasses, side profile” beats “sunglasses”. Prioritise the 125 product pages.
HighOn-page

Every title tag wastes 27 characters on a redundant brand suffix

All 69 pages append – Craycol Active Sunglasses, even when the title already ends in “| Craycol”. 26 titles run past 70 characters and get truncated in results — the longest is 106 characters:

Prescription Sport Sunglasses in India | Complete Guide for Athletes | Craycol – Craycol Active Sunglasses

Fix Remove the theme-level suffix in theme.liquid and keep the hand-written titles, which are already good. Target 55–60 characters.
HighContentIndexation

Two empty blog pages are duplicating your real articles

/blogs/running-without-sunglasses-think-again contains 5 words. /blogs/ironman-70-3-the-journey-beyond-the-finish-line contains 8 words. Both are in the sitemap, both canonicalise to themselves, and both carry the same title as the genuine article at /blogs/news/…. These are blog categories created by accident.

Fix Delete both blog categories in Shopify admin and 301 them to the real articles. They will drop out of the sitemap automatically.
HighOn-page

19 pages have no meta description; 5 say “Design by GemPages”

Nine collections ship with no description at all — including /collections/cricket, /collections/printed, /collections/new and /collections/accessories. Worse, five pages carry a literal page-builder default as their description: /pages/privacy-policy, /pages/cookie-policy, /pages/t-c, and both landing-page-dec-17-21-20-11 and information-page-dec-22-14-27-20.

Four pages also have no <h1>: FAQs, Shipping Policy, Return & Exchange Policy, and the HTML sitemap page.

Fix Write descriptions for the nine collections first — those are commercial pages. Purge the GemPages defaults. Give the FAQ page a real H1; it is a genuine ranking asset for “are Craycol sunglasses prescription-ready” style queries.

Medium-priority fixes

MediumStructured data

No review schema — and no review app to generate it

Zero pages carry aggregateRating. The product pages show a “Hear it from our Community” section, but there is no review platform behind it (no Judge.me, Loox, Yotpo or Okendo detected). Star ratings in search results are one of the largest click-through gains available to a product page, and Google requires them to come from genuine, on-page, verifiable reviews.

Fix Install a review app and collect real reviews via post-purchase email — Klaviyo is already in place to send them. The schema then generates itself. Do not hand-code ratings; fabricated review markup is an explicit structured-data violation.
MediumOn-page

Collection H1s read “Collection: Running”

All 21 collections use the theme default. The title tag for that page is well-written — “Running Sunglasses in India | Lightweight & Antislip” — but the H1 the page actually shows is Collection: Running, which matches no query anyone types.

Fix Change the theme to output the collection title alone, and rename collections to their search-intent phrasing: “Running Sunglasses”, “Cycling Sunglasses”, “Prescription Sport Sunglasses”.
MediumPerformance

Razorpay’s checkout bundle loads on the homepage

The page contacts 39 third-party hosts, including four Razorpay domains, plus cdn.jsdelivr.net, unpkg.com, cdn.nfcube.com and risingsigma.com. Payment scripts only need to exist on cart and checkout. Eight images are also served at more than 2.2× their displayed size.

Fix Gate the Razorpay script to cart/checkout templates. Audit whether the jsDelivr and unpkg dependencies are still used by the live theme — they are frequently left behind by removed apps.
MediumContent gap

You’re invisible for prescription sport eyewear queries

Craycol sells prescription sport sunglasses — there is a /collections/powered-sunglasses collection and RX variants across the catalogue. But a live search for “prescription cycling sunglasses India buy online” returns glassesindia.com, reccy.in and wizbiker.com, with no Craycol result. This is the highest commercial-intent segment of the category and the one where a specialist brand should win.

Fix The article at /blogs/news/prescription-sport-sunglasses-in-india-… is the right asset; it needs internal links pointing at it from every RX product page and from the powered-sunglasses collection. Add a dedicated landing page targeting “prescription cycling sunglasses India” separately from the running variant.

What’s already working

It’s worth being clear about this, because it changes what the priorities are. The foundations here are better than the score suggests — the score is being dragged down almost entirely by page weight.

  • The blog is ranking. “Best Running Sunglasses in India 2026” came back in position 2 on a live search for best running sunglasses India polarised UV400, above Knockaround and several international retailers.
  • Titles and descriptions are written by a human, not generated. “Running Sunglasses in India | Lightweight & Antislip” is a genuinely good title. Most Shopify stores at this size have nothing.
  • Technical hygiene is clean. HTTP→HTTPS and non-www→www both 301 correctly, canonicals are correct on every page, the 404 returns a real 404, and the sitemap structure is valid.
  • Product schema exists on all 125 products with SKU, price, currency, availability and brand — it just needs de-duplicating.
  • Layout stability is excellent — CLS of 0.001 is close to perfect, and that is the CWV metric that is hardest to retrofit.
  • You have real editorial backlinks under the spam: bumsonthesaddle.com and thebikeaffair.com both stock and link to Craycol products, and nextlap.in and aethlete.com are relevant endurance-sport sites. That is the shape the rest of the profile should look like.
  • AI-agent readiness is handled by Shopify — agents.md, UCP/MCP endpoints and an agentic-discovery sitemap are all in place automatically.

What I couldn’t measure

So you know where the edges of this audit are:

  • Keyword rankings and organic traffic volumes. The Semrush plan connected here only authorises the Backlinks API — keyword, position and traffic reports returned authorisation errors. The backlink data above is real Semrush data; there are no keyword volume figures in this report because I could not retrieve any.
  • Real-world Core Web Vitals. No Google API key is configured, so there is no CrUX field data or Search Console data here. All performance numbers are lab measurements from a fast connection — real Indian mobile users will be worse than the 15.8 s figure, not better.
  • 96 of 125 product pages. Shopify’s edge rate-limited my crawler after the first 69 pages. The product template is identical across SKUs, so the on-page findings generalise, but the per-product title and description counts are from a 29-product sample.

The fastest way to close all three gaps: connect Search Console and GA4 to this audit. Search Console alone gives you actual impressions, average positions and click-through rates per query — which is better data than any third-party tool sells, and it is free.
